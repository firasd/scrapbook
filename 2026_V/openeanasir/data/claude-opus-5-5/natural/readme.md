**Obverse**

To Ea-nasir say: Nanni says this:

When you came, you told me, "I will give Gimil-Sin good-quality ingots." Then you left, and you did not do what you promised me. Instead you set out ingots that were not good before my messenger and said, "If you want to take them, take them; if you don't, go away!"

Who do you think I am, that you [...] and treat me with such contempt? I sent gentlemen to someone like you to collect my purse, but you treated me with contempt and sent them back to me empty-handed time and again, through enemy territory.

**Reverse**

Who among the Dilmun traders has ever treated me like this? You have even treated my messenger with contempt! Considering all the silver you took from me, is this how you talk to me?

What is more, on your behalf I personally gave 18 talents of copper to the palace, and Shumi-abum also gave 18 talents of copper, apart from the sealed document we deposited at the temple of Shamash. How have you treated me in return for that copper? You have withheld my purse from me in enemy territory. It is now your responsibility to pay me back in full.

Take note: from now on I will not accept from you any copper that is not of good quality. I will select and take the ingots one by one in my own courtyard.

**Left edge**

And because you have treated me with contempt, I am exercising my right to reject your goods.

---

**Notes**

1. *Obverse, line 17:* The word after "Who do you think I am" is damaged and cannot be restored with confidence, so it is shown as [...].
2. *Obverse, lines 19–21:* "Gentlemen to someone like you" assumes that *ana kīma kâti* refers to the person receiving the messengers. It could also be taken as describing the messengers, as "gentlemen such as yourself." The overall sense of the passage is the same either way.
3. *Obverse, line 23 – reverse, line 2:* "Through enemy territory" can describe either the route the messengers had to travel or the setting of the whole exchange. The phrase *ištiššu u šinīšu*, literally "once and twice," is rendered "time and again."
4. *Reverse, lines 7–9:* This sentence is syntactically difficult. It is taken here as an indignant question about the silver Ea-nasir received. It could also be read as a statement: "This is how you speak about all the silver you took from me."
5. *Reverse, lines 26–50:* The line numbering in the transliteration jumps from 26 to 50, but the text reads as one continuous sentence ("In my courtyard I will select one by one and take…"). It is therefore translated as continuous. If lines are in fact missing, they have not been restored.
6. *Left edge:* *nasīḫtam epēšu* (literally "to make a removal") is understood here as rejecting the goods, or the dealings with Ea-nasir. Its exact legal or commercial force is uncertain.
