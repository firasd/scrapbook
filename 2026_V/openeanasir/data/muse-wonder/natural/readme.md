obverse

Tell Ea-nasir: Nanni says this. When you came, you told me, “I shall give Gimil-Sin ingots of good-quality copper.” Then you went away and failed to do as you had promised me. You set out ingots of poor quality for my messenger and said, “Take them, if you are going to take them; if you are not going to take them, go away!”

Who do you suppose I am, that [—broken—] and you should show me such contempt? I sent gentlemen, men like you, to collect my money-bags, but you contemptuously sent them back to me empty-handed in hostile territory, once and then again.

reverse

Who among those who travel to Dilmun has ever treated me like this? It is you who have treated my messenger contemptuously. You keep talking in this way [concerning] all the silver you have drawn from me.

What is more, on your account I myself gave the palace 18 talents of copper, and Shumi-abum gave 18 talents of copper — quite apart from what we deposited under seal in the temple of Shamash. Why have you behaved like this toward me over that copper? You have detained my money-bags in hostile territory. You are obliged to restore my money-bags to me intact.

Understand this: on the spot I shall not accept copper from you that is not of good quality. In my own courtyard I shall pick out and take them one at a time.

left edge

And in return for the contempt you have shown me, I shall [exercise a right of rejection] against you.

Notes

- The verb in the clause “that [—broken—] and you should show me such contempt” is unreadable in the transliteration, so nothing has been supplied there.
- “Money-bags” renders *kīsū*; the word can denote bags containing silver or the funds themselves, but the claim remains that Ea-nasir withheld property belonging to Nanni.
- The final expression rendered “exercise a right of rejection” is *nasiḫtam epēšum*; its precise legal force is uncertain and could amount to a more general act of repudiation or seizure.
