**Translation Notes**

*   **Obverse 4 (`a-nu-u2-ma ta-al-li-ku`):** Here `a-nu-u2-ma` functions for `inūma` ("when"), governing the subjunctive `talliku` ("you went/came").
*   **Obverse 5, 8–11, 13, 15, 18; Reverse 26, 51:** Words containing signs marked with `#` in the ATF are partially damaged on the tablet but securely readable in context.
*   **Obverse 12–15:** Although Nanni refers to "my messenger" (`mār šiprīya`) in the singular in line 12, the verbs in lines 14–15 (`teleqqea`, `leqea`, `atlaka`) are second-person plural, addressing the messenger and his entourage (the "gentlemen" of line 19).
*   **Obverse 17 (`tu-szi-im-ma ni-x-(x)-x-x-ma`):** The verbal form `tu-szi-im-ma` is difficult (often taken as an unusual vocalization of `tašīm-ma` from `šâmu`, "to reckon, consider"), and the following word (`ni-x-(x)-x-x-ma`) is too broken to translate beyond the possible first-person plural prefix `ni-` and enclitic `-ma`.
*   **Obverse 20, Reverse 19, 21 (`ki-si-ia` / `ki-si`):** Literally "my money-bag" (`kīsum`), referring to the leather pouch of silver used as business capital for the partnership.
*   **Reverse 7 (`a-na ma-<la> ka-as-pi-im`):** The ATF restores `<la>` where the scribe omitted a sign (`ana mala kaspim`, literally "for as much silver as..."). Some scholars alternatively emend `ma` to `mi-<ṣi>` ("a trifle of silver").
*   **Reverse 26, 50–51:** The line numbering jumps from 26 to 50 to match the numbering of the supplied ATF.
*   **Left edge 2 (`na-si-ih-ta-am`):** A noun derived from `nasāhu` ("to tear out, remove, deduct, reject"). Its exact commercial or legal nuance here is uncertain; it likely refers to exercising a right of rejection, exacting a financial deduction, or imposing a penalty.
