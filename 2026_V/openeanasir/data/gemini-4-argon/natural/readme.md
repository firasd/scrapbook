**obverse**
Speak to Ea-nāṣir; thus says Nanni:

When you came here, you made this statement to me: "I will deliver fine-quality ingots to Gimil-Sîn." You came and made me that promise, yet you did not follow through. Instead, you set out ingots of poor quality before my messenger and declared: "If you want to take them, take them! If you do [not] want to take them, leave!"

What sort of person have you taken me for, that [...] and you treat me with such disdain? I dispatched men of standing like yourself to collect my purse, yet you showed only disdain and, across hostile territory,

**reverse**
sent them back to me empty-handed once and even twice! Among the traders who travel to Dilmun, who has ever acted this way toward me? You alone have disrespected my messenger!

For all the silver that you have gotten out of my hands, you dare to speak like this—while on your behalf I myself delivered 18 talents of copper to the palace, and Šumi-abum likewise delivered 18 talents of copper, quite apart from what we recorded on the sealed document that we deposited in the temple of Šamaš.

How have you treated me over that copper! You have held back my purse in hostile territory. You are now obligated to restore my purse to my hands in full. Be aware that here I will not accept from you any copper that is not of good quality. From now on, I shall select the ingots one by one in my own courtyard before I take them.

**left edge**
And because you have treated me with disdain, I shall enforce my right of rejection against you!

***

**Notes**
* **Obverse 14–15:** The verbs rendered "want to take," "take," and "leave" (*teleqqeā*, *leqeā*, *atlakā*) are grammatically plural, indicating that Ea-nāṣir is addressing both the messenger (singular in line 12) and his accompanying party. In line 15, `[not]` translates the restored negative particle `[la]`.
* **Obverse 17:** The sequence `ni-x-(x)-x-x-ma` is broken and illegible on the tablet; it originally supplied a coordinated verb or phrase between *tušīmma* ("you reckoned/took me for") and *kīam temêšanni* ("you treat me with such disdain").
* **Obverse 20, Reverse 19, 21:** Literally "my bag/purse" (*kīsī*), which in Old Babylonian commercial correspondence refers both to the physical leather pouch and, by metonymy, to the silver or working capital stored inside it.
* **Reverse 7–8:** The transliteration reads `a-na ma-<la> ka-as-pi-im` ("for all the silver / the amount of silver"). Some commentators alternatively emend the second sign to `ma-<na>` ("for a [single] mina of silver"). Additionally, *ša ina qātīya tušēṣû* ("which you got out of my hands") can be understood either as silver Ea-nāṣir had already extracted from Nanni as payment, or as funds Ea-nāṣir caused Nanni to lose.
* **Left edge 2:** The idiom *nasihtam epēšum* (from *nasāhum*, "to tear out, cull, deduct, remove") is rare; depending on context, it denotes either subjecting Ea-nāṣir's deliveries to ruthless culling/rejection or imposing a punitive financial deduction upon him.
