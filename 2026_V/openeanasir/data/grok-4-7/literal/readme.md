Obverse

(1–3) To Ea-nāṣir say: thus Nanni:

(4–8) Now, you came, (and) thus you [said to me] — thus you (said): “I will [give] good-quality ingots to Gimil-Sîn.”

(9–10) You went away, and you [said] (this) to me, (but) you did not [do] (it).

(11–15) Ingots that were not [good] you [set] before my messenger, and thus you (said): “If you (pl.) are going to take (them), take (them); if you are [not] going to [take] (them), go away.”

(16–18) As whom have you reckoned me? You have regarded me (so), and […], (and) thus you have [treated me with contempt].

(19–23) Gentlemen like you I sent to receive my money-bag, but you treated me with contempt, and through enemy territory,

Reverse

(1–2) once and twice, you returned them to me empty-handed.

(3–6) Who is there, among those who go to Telmun, who has done such a thing to me? You yourself have treated my messenger with contempt.

(7–9) On account of the <amount of> silver which you caused to be taken out of my hand, you speak thus;

(10–14) and on your account I myself gave 18 talents of copper to the palace, and Šumi-abum gave 18 talents of copper,

(15–16) apart from (the fact) that we deposited a sealed tablet for the temple of Šamaš.

(17–18) How have you treated me with regard to that copper?

(19–20) You have withheld my money-bag in enemy territory.

(21–22) It is incumbent upon you to restore my money-bag safely into my hand.

(23–25) You will learn that here I will not accept from you copper that is not of good quality.

(26, 50–51) [In] my courtyard I will select (them) one by one, and [I will take] (them).

Left edge

(1–2) And because you treated me with contempt, I will impose a deduction upon you.

Notes

1. Parentheses mark words added only for English sense. Square brackets mark restored text, readings that rest on signs flagged as damaged in the ATF (#), or, as […], signs the ATF leaves illegible (obv. 17: ni-x-(x)-x-x-ma), which are not restored. Angle brackets mark an editorial supplement.

2. Obv. 15: [not] translates [la], supplied in the ATF and not preserved on the tablet. The verbs in obv. 14–15 are plural, although “my messenger” (obv. 12) is singular.

3. “Ingots” translates gubāru (obv. 7, 11), a trade term for the copper pieces at issue; its exact technical sense is uncertain. damqu is “good” in the commercial sense, “of good quality.”

4. kīsu (obv. 20; rev. 19, 21) is the merchant’s money-bag or capital. Telmun is the name written on the tablet (Dilmun). Gimil-Sîn is written with {d}SUEN; Šamaš is the Akkadian name of the god written {d}UTU. “18 talents” renders 1(u) 8(diš) gú; no modern weight equivalent is given here.

5. Rev. 7: the tablet has a-na ma-ka-as-pi-im; <la> is editorial, giving ana ma<la> kaspim, “for the amount of the silver.” The translation does not add any quantity or debt not written in the ATF.

6. The supplied ATF numbers the reverse through line 26 and then 50–51, with no lines 27–49 given; nothing is restored in that gap. Rev. 26 and 51 are read from damaged signs.

7. Left edge 2: e-pu-usz-ka may be read as future “I will do” or past “I have done”; it is taken as future to match the preceding verbs. nasihtu is literally a “deduction” or “withdrawal,” in this commercial context the right to reject or deduct for unsatisfactory goods.
