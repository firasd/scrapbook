### obverse

Say to Ea-nāṣir: thus says Nanni:

When you came, you [said to me] as follows: “I shall [give] good ingots to Gimil-Sîn.” You went away, but you did not [do] what you [said to me]. You [set] ingots that were not [good] before my messenger and said: “If you will take them, take them; if you [will not take them], go away.”

What sort of person do you take me for, that […] you [treat me with contempt] like this? I sent men of standing to someone like you to receive my money-bag. You treated them with contempt and, in hostile territory,

### reverse

once and twice you sent them back to me empty-handed.

Who among those who go to Dilmun has treated me like this? Yet you have treated my messenger with contempt. Over [the amount of] silver that you [take] from my hands, you speak like this!

And on your account, I myself gave the palace eighteen talents of copper, and Šumi-abum gave eighteen talents of copper, apart from that for which we left a sealed document at the temple of Šamaš.

How have you treated me concerning that copper? You have held back my money-bag in hostile territory. I have a claim against you for the return of my money-bag intact into my hands.

You will learn that I will not accept copper that is not good from you here. [In] my courtyard I shall select the ingots one by one and [take them].

### left edge

And because you have treated me with contempt, I shall reject ingots from you.

### Notes

- Bracketed words mark damaged readings, supplied text, or uncertain interpretation. […] marks the unreadable passage in obverse 17; no wording has been restored there. The negative “not” in obverse 15 is supplied in the ATF.
- “Men of standing” renders literally “sons of a gentleman.” “Money-bag” translates *kīsum*, which can also refer to the funds it contains.
- “[The amount of]” follows the supplied ATF’s *ma-<la>*, with *la* editorially inserted. The following verb literally means “cause to come out of my hands”; whether the silver has already been taken or remains to be collected is uncertain.
- The final clause literally says, “I shall make a rejection against you.” “Ingots” supplies the object understood from the preceding discussion, not text restored in a gap.
