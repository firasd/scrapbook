# OpenEaNasir 𒂍𒀀𒈾𒍢𒅕

About 4,000 years ago, a man named Nanni wrote a complaint to Ea-nāṣir, a copper merchant who&mdash;apparently&mdash;lacked customer service skills.

Wikipedia recounts the sordid affair:

<blockquote>
According to Nanni's complaint tablet, Nanni entrusted Ea-nāṣir with money in advance for the purposes of acquiring an agreed-upon amount of copper of sufficient quality. However, when Nanni sent a servant through hostile territory to acquire the ingots from Ea-nāṣir, he presented Nanni's servant with poor-quality ingots while mistreating and undermining him, and stated the equivalent of "take it or leave it" in a dismissive manner.

&mdash; [wikipedia.org/wiki/Ea-nāṣir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ea-n%C4%81%E1%B9%A3ir)
</blockquote>

While this story from 1750 B.C. has become an internet meme, I couldn&rsquo;t find an English translation released into the public domain. Wikipedia&rsquo;s [Complaint tablet to Ea-nāṣir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complaint_tablet_to_Ea-n%C4%81%E1%B9%A3ir) page doesn&rsquo;t have the full text.

Nanni, who was much put-upon in life, was still subdued in death.

Until now.

[OpenEaNasir](https://github.com/firasd/scrapbook/tree/main/2026_V/openeanasir) is releasing English translations of the tablet, produced independently by AI models based on [CDLI’s ATF transliteration](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/414985).

## Natural translation, by Grok 4.7

obverse

To Ea-nāṣir — message from Nanni.

When you came, you told me, in these words: “I will give Gimil-Sîn good ingots.” You left having said that, and you did not do it. You set ingots of poor quality before my messenger and said, “If you are going to take them, take them. If you are [not] going to take them, go.” Who have you taken me for? [several signs missing], and this is how you have treated me with contempt. I sent men of good family, men like yourself, to receive my purse. You treated them with contempt and, in enemy territory,

reverse

once and a second time, you sent them back to me empty-handed. Who among those who trade with Dilmun has ever done this to me? You have treated my messenger with contempt. And this is how you speak about the silver you took out of my hands. On your account I myself gave the palace eighteen talents of copper, and Šumi-abum gave eighteen talents of copper — quite apart from the sealed document we deposited with the temple of Shamash. How could you treat me this way over that copper? You have held my purse in enemy territory. It is your duty to see my purse safely into my hands. You will learn that I will not accept poor-quality copper from you here. In my courtyard I will sort it piece by piece and take what I choose.

left edge

And because you have treated me with contempt, I will exact a deduction from you.
